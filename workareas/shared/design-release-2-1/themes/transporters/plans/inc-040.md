## 0. Decisions

**The four criteria were checked against the branch, not taken on trust.** All four are unmet, exactly as the
6 November re-analysis found against `main`:

1. `copy.en.js:212` reads `Add private transporter` — the article is missing, so the heading and the browser
   title both fall short. The caption is already `Add a new transporter`
   (`flow/section-captions/copy/copy.en.js:14`, bound to the private page at `flow/section-captions/index.js:48`),
   and the layout already composes the browser title from the page title, the service name and the GOV.UK suffix
   (`shared/layout.njk:18`), so changing the one copy value satisfies both halves of the first criterion.
2. An intro paragraph sits between the heading and the name field (`private-transporter-details.njk:13`, copy at
   `copy.en.js:213`).
3. The select opens on an empty placeholder followed by a disabled divider rule, and `addressCountries()` puts the
   United Kingdom first and then whatever order reference data served
   (`private-transporter-details.controller.js:93-101`; `services/countries/index.js:111-114`).
4. The mandatory checks sit behind `recordProvided(values)`, which is false when every entered field is blank, so
   a wholly blank form returns no errors and walks on (`private-transporter-details.controller.js:81-91`).

**Where the select's order lives: `addressCountries()`, not the page.** The third criterion is literally about the
line that prepends the United Kingdom and keeps reference data's order, and that line is in the countries service.
Sorting anywhere else would leave the service asserting one order and the page overriding it — two owners for one
rule inside the one repo. The service already sorts its other select-facing list with the same comparator
(`services/countries/index.js:96-109` using `country-labels.js:4`), so this extends the mechanism that is there.
Rejected: sorting inside the controller's option builder, which would have kept the change inside the feature but
left the prepend in place and split the rule across two files.

**The live twin of that ordering rule is the INS front door, and this row leaves it holding the rule too.**
`repos/trade-imports-ins-frontend/src/server/app/features/address-book/address-countries.js:11-25` already owns
exactly the rule this row gives the animals countries service: it reads reference data's countries, drops the
United Kingdom's own code, and answers `[GB_COUNTRY, ...withoutGb].toSorted(byName)` — the United Kingdom
together with every served country, in label order, so the United Kingdom falls in its alphabetical place
(`:24`, with its comparator at `:9` and its United Kingdom record at `:7`). After this row the same rule is
owned by two live functions in two repos, each with its own United Kingdom record and its own comparator. That is
duplication, not a behaviour difference: both answer the same list in the same order. It is recorded as a
departure in section 14 and a follow-up in section 7, and it is not resolved here because the row's repos are the
animals frontend and the tests repo, and the INS front door's own add-a-transporter pages are carried by the rows
section 7 names (inc-074 to inc-076, with inc-265 for approved transporters) under the 6 November ruling that
makes the address book the system of record. Rejected: extracting one shared owner now, which needs a change in
`insfrontend`, a repo this row does not list and whose transporter area those rows are actively moving; and
rejected: returning `ok:false`, because the criteria describe the animals pages, the gate proves the animals
pages, and a recorded duplicate of a rule that already agrees is a departure to carry, not a blocker.

**The live twin of this page is the INS front door's private transporter form, and after this row the two forms
disagree on two criteria.** That form is already titled `Add a private transporter`
(`features/address-book/copy/copy.en.js:145`) — the heading the first criterion asks for, which the animals page
gains here, so the two agree on criterion 1 afterwards. They will differ on the other two:

- **Criterion 2, what comes first under the heading.** The INS form puts an address sub-heading between its `h1`
  and its name field (`features/address-book/transporter/private-transporter.njk:10-15`, copy at
  `copy/copy.en.js:146`). The animals page's name field becomes the first thing under the heading.
- **Criterion 3, the country select.** The INS form builds its select through
  `buildCountrySelectItems(countries, copy.countryPlaceholder)`
  (`features/address-book/transporter/private-transporter.controller.js:73`, builder at
  `address-countries.js:33-36`, copy at `copy/copy.en.js:160`), so it keeps a `Select a country` placeholder as
  its opening option and has no United Kingdom default. The animals select loses its placeholder and opens on the
  United Kingdom.

Decision: the animals pages change and the INS form is left as it is, for the same reason as the ordering rule —
the row's repos, and those two differences being design questions for the form that the add-a-transporter rows
own, on the route that is replacing the journey-local forms. Both differences are recorded in section 14 as a
departure with their reason, and in section 7 as the rows that converge the two forms. Rejected: changing the INS
form's sub-heading order and its placeholder here, which decides a second service's design inside a row scoped to
one, before the row that owns where these pages live has landed.

**Where the select's default lives: the function that builds the options.** Criterion 3 wants United Kingdom chosen
on open; the error re-render and the stale-country path need the same answer, because once the placeholder goes
there is no option that can represent "nothing". One function — the one that builds the select's items — decides
which option opens chosen: the country in hand when it is one of the offered countries, otherwise the United
Kingdom. `values` therefore stays the honest record of what was stored or entered (a stale country still shows as
blank in `values`, with its own error), and no other test's "show what they entered" assertion moves. Rejected:
rewriting `values.country` to the United Kingdom in the GET and the refusal path, which would make the shared
"a refused save shows what was entered" helper disagree with itself about the country.

**A tampered or absent country still has its own refusal.** The design source has no blank option, so this case
cannot arise from the page itself (`prototype-dr2-1-source` records that the "Select a country" error cannot
normally appear). It is still reachable from a crafted post, and the commercial form already refuses a tampered
country rather than correcting it, so the private form keeps its country-required message and the select shows the
United Kingdom beside the error. The row's own note settles the same point: drop the placeholder's copy key, keep
the country-required error.

**Required-ness moves into the validation schema.** Once the blank-form guard goes, the page's hand-rolled
mandatory-message map duplicates what `lib/validate` already provides — `requiredMaxText` for save-blocking capped
text and `requiredOneOf` for a save-blocking value domain, both exported from
`lib/validate/index.js:3-25`, both documented as existing precisely because composing an optional rule with a
required one lets a blank through (`lib/validate/validators.js:66-76`). The private page's mandatory fields take
those two rules and the local map goes. Rejected: keeping the local map and merely deleting the guard, which would
leave a second required-ness mechanism beside the library's.

**The six messages of criterion 4, and where the country sits in that order.** A blank form posted from the page
carries the United Kingdom in its country field, because the select has no empty option to post, so the country
does not error and the summary reads the six messages the criterion lists. The page's field order keeps the
country in its own place between the postcode and the email, which is where the part-filled refusal already
reports it (`private-transporter-details.controller.test.js:123-134`) — that order is unchanged, and only a post
that omits or blanks the country reaches it.

**The commercial form and the shared save are left alone.** The identical guard, map and walk-on branch also serve
the commercial form (`commercial-transporter-details.controller.js:97-107`,
`transporter-details-save.js:91-120`), which still accepts a blank record until inc-041 lands. Extracting a shared
helper now would create something inc-041 then deletes; so the private form converts, the commercial one is
untouched, and the duplication disappears when inc-041 converts too. `recordProvided` therefore stays in the
private controller as the shared save's option. Rejected: changing the shared save in this row.

**Plants is not changed.** The high-risk plants frontend holds a twin `addressCountries()`
(`repos/trade-imports-plants-frontend/src/server/app/services/countries/index.js:117-120`) with no production
caller, and no private transporter page at all, so nothing a plants user sees changes and the programme's
plants-mirror invariant does not bite. Section 7 carries the mirror as a follow-up for the row that gives plants
an address-country select.

**Two end-to-end specs change because the blank private save stops working, not for tidiness.**
`tests/animals/e2e/features/commercial-transporter-scope.spec.ts:29-31` uses a blank private save as its way of
switching the type and landing back on Overview; it must complete the private form instead.
`tests/animals/e2e/features/private-transporter-scope.spec.ts` asserts the country is blank after the type is
switched back, which becomes the United Kingdom default.

**The new real transporter save gets the shared cleanup, and a run-unique name.** The address book inserts a row
per save: `TransporterService.create` maps the request to a new entity, stamps it and calls `repository.save`
with no lookup by name
(`repos/trade-imports-address-book/.../transporter/TransporterService.java:111-119`), and the animals list's
"one name means one row" join only replaces a *shipped* record with an added one of the same name
(`services/transporters/index.js:35-43`), so two added rows under one name sit side by side on the list. The
commercial-scope spec's new private save therefore uses the canned record under a name made unique for the run,
looks the saved record up by that name and registers its id with the tests repo's shared cleanup registrar
(section 11). Rejected: a fixed name with no cleanup, which leaves a row per run for that organisation and
eventually two identical rows the list's radios cannot tell apart. The private-scope spec's existing save already
accumulates under a fixed name; that is pre-existing and outside this row's behaviour, and section 7 carries it
as a follow-up rather than adding a cleanup call site this row does not need.

### frontend — `.../features/transport/private-transporter-details/private-transporter-details.controller.js`

Four things change, each with one owner:

- **The field schema owns required-ness.** The six mandatory text fields take the library's save-blocking capped
  text rule with their existing required and max-length messages; the country takes the library's save-blocking
  value-domain rule over `addressCountries()`, with the country-required message for a blank or absent value and
  the existing out-of-list message for an unoffered one. Address line 2 and county stay optional capped text. The
  local mandatory-message map and the function that applied it behind the blank-form guard both go, and the
  function that assembles the page's errors then needs only the payload — it keeps re-ordering the errors by the
  page's field order, country included in its own place, so a blank form posted from the page reads name, address
  line 1, town or city, postcode, email, phone.
- **The option builder owns which option opens chosen**, by the rule set out in section 0: the country in hand
  when it is offered, otherwise the United Kingdom, read from the countries service. The empty placeholder item
  and the disabled divider item go, so every option is a country name.
- **The GET keeps the stale-country rule as it is**: a stored or remembered country reference data no longer
  offers is shown as blank in the page's values and carries its own error. What changes is only what the select
  displays, and that is the option builder's business now.
- **`recordProvided` stays** as the shared save's option, unchanged (section 0, section 15).

  (`~/git/defra/trade-imports-workspace/.claude/rules/code-quality.md`, Structure.) The offered-countries ordering
  rule ends up owned twice over, by two live functions: `addressCountries()` in the animals frontend as this row
  changes it, and `getAddressFormCountries()` in the INS front door
  (`repos/trade-imports-ins-frontend/src/server/app/features/address-book/address-countries.js:11-25`), which

  Reason: the row's repos are the animals frontend and the tests repo; the INS front door's transporter area is
  being moved by the add-a-transporter rows inc-074 to inc-076 (with inc-265 for approved transporters) under
  ruling:ian-2026-11-06, which makes the address book the system of record for transporters, so the one owner is
  extracted there rather than being guessed at now. The two functions agree on the answer, so no user sees a
  difference. Section 7 carries the extraction as a follow-up.
- **"Extend the mechanism that exists. Do not add a second one beside it."** and **"Build copy, layout and
  components as the design source sets them"** (same file, Structure and Design.) After this row the two live
  private transporter forms ask the same question in two ways. The animals form leads with its name field and
  opens its Country select on the United Kingdom; the INS front door's form keeps an address sub-heading between
  its heading and its name field (`features/address-book/transporter/private-transporter.njk:10-15`) and a

  this row builds, and whether the INS form should follow is a design question for the form the add-a-transporter
  rows above are rehoming; deciding it here would change a second service's pages from a row scoped to one. Both
  are raised for the designer as follow-ups in section 7. They agree on the heading itself, which both forms read
  as `Add a private transporter` once this row lands.
- **"Write no worker count into a plan: whoever runs the check adds this machine's `--workers`"** (this run's
  plan-writing brief). Section 5's FIT and end-to-end commands carry this machine's counts (`--workers=8` for the
  animals frontend's `fit` rung, `--workers=2` for the tests repo's `e2e` rung). Reason: the run's standing
